System overview

Designed for safety, performance, and clarity.

SafeBeat is engineered to track real-time cardiovascular signals during physical activity. Embedded sensors and an ESP32 processing unit continuously analyse heart-rate patterns and blood-oxygen levels to identify early warning signs of cardiac stress.

Designed for athletes, SafeBeat transforms raw biometric data into actionable insights, helping users notice potential risks before they become danger.

01

Unified monitoring ecosystem

Secure, real-time synchronisation connects the wearable vest, mobile interface, and web dashboard. Athletes and coaches can visualise live metrics, review historical data, and monitor performance from anywhere.

The problem

The most dangerous threat is the one you do not see.

Sudden cardiac events in young athletes are rare, but when they occur, they are often unexpected. Most consumer fitness trackers measure surface-level metrics such as heart rate, but they do not analyse deeper cardiovascular patterns or abnormal rhythm variations during intense physical stress.

Without continuous signal monitoring, early warning signs can remain unnoticed. SafeBeat was created to bridge that gap by providing intelligent, real-time analysis of heart-rhythm patterns and oxygen saturation during performance.

High-intensity riskPhysical exertion can increase cardiovascular strain, especially with undiagnosed conditions.
Real-time monitoringContinuous signal analysis helps identify abnormal rhythm patterns during activity.

The prototype

Engineered for safety and performance.

SafeBeat integrates medical monitoring sensors with an ESP32 microcontroller to acquire and process cardiovascular signals in real time. The system captures electrical heart activity while simultaneously monitoring oxygen-saturation levels during physical exertion.

Its lightweight, impact-resistant module is designed to attach seamlessly to compression apparel. Optimised dry-contact electrodes and signal-filtering algorithms help maintain stable readings during high-intensity movement.
